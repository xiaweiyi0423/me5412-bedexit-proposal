Synthetic data: about 500 normal exits and 500 falls; falls from an inverted-pendulum model, so results are optimistic. Next: volunteers fall on soft mats + older adults step off normally; continue only if detection ≥ 80% and false alarms ≤ 5 / 100

Second fall question: if someone falls while stepping off the footplate after use, can we tell? We add no new sensors. We only use the four load cells in the footplate and the force sensing in the grab bar. In a normal step-off, the force leaves the feet slowly, and the centre of pressure stays in the middle of the standing foot. In a fall, the force goes faster, the centre of pressure first moves to the foot edge, and if the legs give way, the knees hit the footplate. No single signal is enough. Combined, on our generated data, at five false alarms per hundred normal exits, we detect about 98% of falls. Under very bad conditions, about 80%. Two points. First, we made this data with a model, so the result is optimistic. We must test real people: volunteers falling on soft mats, and older adults stepping off normally. We continue only at 80% detection and at most five false alarms per hundred. Second, detection can only raise an alarm, turn on lights and call family. It cannot stop this fall. A fall after the user has left the footplate is not seen, and is out of our scope.
