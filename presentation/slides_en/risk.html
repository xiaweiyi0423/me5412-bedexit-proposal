4 · SAFETY

Six risks: where they occur and how each is guarded

1

Pinched hands or legs · 3 cm gap

Seat edge 3 cm from housing wall; bracket exits via a brush-sealed slot; padded rear edge; stops if lowering meets resistance (motor current or seat-force jump)

2

Sliding off the seat · tilt ≤ 16°

Max 15.7° over the stroke; high-friction fabric; no lift if CoP leaves the safe zone

5

Power loss, jamming · max 3 cm/s

Power-off brake holds; battery lowering by holding "Down"; if the controller fails, a covered lever under the bar corner (cuts the motor first) lowers slowly via a centrifugal speed limiter

6

Frame pulled over by a grab · tips at 156 N

Fixed to the bed (empty frame tips at about 156 N pushed toward the bed head); bar end rounded, padded, glows at night

Not at one part: control logic

3

Dizzy, sitting back while lifting: speed → 0, stops to catch; lowers only while "Down" is held; long sitting → reminder + family call only

4

Sensor failure: zero check when empty; force sum vs body weight off 15–35% → slow + voice prompt, >35% → lock-out; dual-channel switch

Sideways falls: next slide. Fall detection off the footplate: appendix. Reference standards: IEC 60601-1-11, ISO 13482, EN 12182

The numbers on the side view match the locations. One: the seat edge is 3 centimetres from the housing wall, the rear edge is padded, and it stops if blocked. Two: forward tilt stays under 16 degrees, with high-friction fabric. Five: the motor has a power-off brake; in a power cut, a battery or the lever under the bar lowers it slowly. Six: the frame is fixed to the bed, and the bar end is padded and glows at night. Three, dizziness and sitting back, and four, sensor failure, are control logic: stop and catch the user; if the self-check fails, slow down first, then lock out.
