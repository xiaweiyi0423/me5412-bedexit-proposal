APPENDIX · HOW IT IS USED

Bed-Exit Seat: one complete use in five steps

1 Sit on the bed edge; lean forward to reach the grab bar

Seat 0.42 m, flush with the bed

2 Hold the bar, shift onto the bed-level seat; sit as long as needed

Seat 0.42 m · tilt 1°

3 Hold the “Up” grip switch: seat moves forward, up and tilts with the thighs

0.48 m · tilt 9° · peak 16 kPa

4 User pushes too; cushion supports hips and upper thighs

0.54 m · tilt 15° · peak 14 kPa

5 Stand steady; seat holds where released and still catches the user who sits back

Stops at 0.57 m, where released

No changes to the bed. Frame along the long side, fixed to the bed. All mechanism in a closed housing at the head end; foot end open, so legs can swing round on return. L-shaped grab bar fixed to the base; bar right in front of the user.

Sketches drawn from the 2D model (1.60 m, 60 kg), not CAD; red = cushion–body contact; kPa = peak cushion pressure

Our design is the Bed-Exit Seat. It stands along the long side of the bed. The bed is not changed, and the frame is fixed to it. One use has five steps. First, sit on the bed edge and lean forward to hold the grab bar. Second, pull on the bar, shift onto a seat level with the bed, and sit as long as you need. Third, hold the “Up” grip switch. The seat does not push straight up: it moves forward and up with the thighs, and tilts forward a little. Fourth, the user also pushes, and the cushion supports the hips and upper thighs. Fifth, once standing, the seat stops where you let go, so it still catches a dizzy sit-back. The mechanism sits in a closed housing at the head end, and the foot end is open for the legs.
