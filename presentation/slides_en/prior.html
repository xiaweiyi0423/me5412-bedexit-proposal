2 · EXISTING SOLUTIONS

We borrow proven safety ideas, but keep the bed as it is

Lift chairs, toilet seat risers

Whole chair or seat ring lifts; 15–30 s

Borrow: hold-to-run; battery lowering in a power cut. Differ: no new furniture

Raised bed + grab bar

Bed legs raised to about 0.58 m; cheapest

Differ: bed height capped by flat feet when seated; our seat rises only as you rise. Compared next

Gas-spring cushion (UpEasy)

No power; pushes up slowly as you start to rise

Differ: push can be capped; seat follows the thighs, up to 0.69 m

Research chairs, patents

Univ. of Tokyo (Kogami et al.): seat CoP detects intent to rise. Panasonic: lifts only with armrest force + seat unloading

Borrow: force interlock; speed follows force

Bed-Exit Seat

Stands by the bed, bed unchanged; seat moves forward, up and tilts with the thighs

One motor; mechanism at the bed-head side, foot end open

First four: side-view sketches (not to scale; orange = moving parts); last: our CAD model. Patents: US10022284, US9962305, US5619762

Lift chairs and toilet seat risers can lift a person. We borrowed two ideas: it moves only while you hold the button, and a battery lowers you in a power cut. But they need new furniture. The cheapest option is a raised bed with a grab bar; we compare it next. A gas-spring cushion cannot cap its push. Research chairs and a Panasonic patent use seat and armrest forces to detect that someone wants to stand; we borrowed this force interlock. Our difference: it stands beside the bed, the bed is unchanged, and the seat follows the thighs.
