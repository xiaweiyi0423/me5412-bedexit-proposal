4 · SENSING & SPEED

Grip switch commands; four load cells set the speed and run self-checks

▶ Film clip · loops

v = k × (push limit − seat force), clamped between 0 and max speed

Four sensing points

  1. Under the seat: seated and centred? (CoP); how much push
  2. Grab bar: hands on? Hanging on the bar → speed limited
  3. Grip switch: hold to rise, release to stop
  4. Footplate: feet flat? Speeds up only when force leaving the seat shows up here

Speed follows the user

  • More weight off the seat → faster; sit back → speed 0, never reverses
  • Personal settings: push limit 0.5–0.6 × body weight, max 1.5–3 cm/s
  • All 16 simulated scenarios end safe; typical user leaves the seat at 0.57 m after about 7 s (appendix)
  • Self-check: zero when empty; while lifting, sum of three forces ≈ body weight

No weighing under the base: once fixed to the bed, part of the load goes to the bed, so the base can't measure it. Precedent: Univ. of Tokyo (Kogami et al.) assist chair uses seat CoP to detect intent to rise

Control has two parts. The command: hold the switch on the grab bar to rise; let go, and it stops. Force sensing does two jobs. First, speed. Speed equals k times the push limit minus the seat force. The more the user pushes, the faster it rises; if they sit back, speed drops to zero. Force leaving the seat must show up on the footplate before it speeds up, and hanging on the bar limits the speed. Second, self-check: the three forces should add up to about body weight. All 16 simulated scenarios end safe. A typical user leaves the seat at 0.57 metres after about 7 seconds.
