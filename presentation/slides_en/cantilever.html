APPENDIX · HOW THE ONE-SIDED CANTILEVER HOLDS

Seat hangs from the head end only: a bearing pair at pivot 1 takes the moment, then the carriage plate passes it to twin linear guides

Worst case: 100 kg × 1.5 body weight, on the open-side edge (10 cm offset), plus 0.3 × side push

  • Pivot 1 moment 586 N·m: two 32006 X tapered roller bearings, 45 mm apart, turn it into push–pull; equivalent static load 13.6 kN, static SF 3.2
  • Shaft Ø30 (42CrMo4, Q&T): 208 MPa, allowable 433
  • Beam 50×30×4 (S355): 113 MPa, allowable 237
  • 4 HGH15CA carriages: worst one 5.2 kN, static SF 2.5
  • Seat outer edge total sag about 4.0 mm: target ≤ 5 mm

All hand calculations (static equilibrium + beam formulas), not FEA yet; bearing and carriage ratings from distributor data, to be checked against maker catalogues. Part numbers: see appendix drawings

Reviewers often ask: the seat hangs from one side only, so will it bend or twist? This page answers that. On the left is a section, seen from the foot end. A user sitting on the open-side edge is far from the support. This makes a moment of about 586 newton-metres at pivot 1. At pivot 1 we use a back-to-back pair of tapered roller bearings, 45 millimetres apart, in a 75 millimetre bearing housing welded to the carriage plate. The pair turns the moment into a push and a pull. The worst bearing sees about 13.6 kilonewtons, static safety factor 3.2. We first looked at a crossed roller bearing. But one strong enough is 120 millimetres across and leaves only 0.9 centimetres to the mattress, so we changed to the tapered pair. The shaft, beam and carriages all pass the same worst case. Total edge sag is about 4 millimetres. These are hand calculations; next, our CAD and FEA members will check them.
