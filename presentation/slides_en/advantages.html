The three products each solve one part: something to hold, a push from a seat, or a whole chair that lifts. Our design puts together use at the bed edge, support under the hips and thighs, and motion forward and up. When an older adult rises from the bed edge, the hips first move forward, then rise; the seat moves along that direction. That is our main ergonomic idea. The model gives first evidence. Effort: peak leg effort divided by body weight, median over 81 parameter sets, is about a third lower than from a low bed, about the same as a raised bed. Catch: if the legs give way and the hips drop straight down, the seat is under them for the whole rise; falling backward, 73 percent. Fit: 80 of 81 parameter sets pass the path check, against 11 for a vertical lift. The 81 are parameter sets, not 81 people, and all of this comes from a 2D quasi-static model; it still has to be tested on a prototype with people.
