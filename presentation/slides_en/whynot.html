This is the key slide. To be honest: on effort alone, a bed raised to 0.58 metres with a grab bar works well. We need about a third less effort than a low bed, but only about 10% less than a raised bed, and 22 users find it slightly harder. So effort is about equal. The real difference is when the legs give way. At about 70% of the rise, for both beds the hips land about 16 centimetres past the edge, about 25 when upright. The vertical seat only goes up: they land 5 centimetres in front of it. Only the curved seat is still underneath. Beds catch you only in the first 15% or so, vertical lift 43%, our seat all the way; if the knees buckle, 73%. In a stiff backward fall the bed is better, so we pad the rear edge.
