3 · STABILITY

Seated users can't tip it; a push from the floor can, so it is fixed to the bed

User on the seat: pulling the bar is an internal force; the ground-reaction point stays inside the base

User on the floor: an external force; 156 N (about 16 kgf) toward the bed head puts the point on the edge → it tips

Fixed to the bed; fixings sized for 2211 N

Grab-bar design load 1112 N × 1.5 on the bar

Locked to the bed frame or legs (a plate under the mattress can't hold 2.2 kN); frame mass estimated from part sizes

Base x 3–78 cm (away from the bed), y -27 … +38 cm (65 cm along the bed); housing at the bed-head side, frame centre of mass 20 cm toward the bed head (estimated from part sizes)

This is the top view. The black outline is the base, the support polygon. The dots are ground-reaction points, not the centre of mass. With the user on the frame, pulling the bar is an internal force. A 100 kg user dropping onto the seat's rear: still 9 centimetres from the rear edge. 45 kg: 16 centimetres. The whole body hanging on the bar: 23 centimetres from the bed-head side. All inside. The danger is a person on the floor holding the bar. The frame is only about 85 kg. Push 156 newtons toward the bed head, about 16 kilograms of force, and it starts to tip. So we fix it to the bed. With the grab-bar design load, 1112 newtons times 1.5, the fixings take 2211 newtons, locked to the bed frame or legs.
