Fixes ①–③ are concepts, not yet modelled. 156 N: our tipping check (frame 84.6 kg, push at the bar toward the bed head); ≈ 400 N is an estimate from the same check with a wider base

Last, our limits and how the next version answers each one. One: the frame weighs about 85 kilograms and is clamped to one bed. Next: casters to move it, and a floor lock; the seat start height is set to the bed, or even a sofa. One caution from our own check: a wheel lock stops rolling, not tipping. With nobody on it, a push of about 156 newtons at the bar toward the bed head tips the frame. So the lock should also put out a foot about 30 centimetres toward the bed head, which raises that to roughly 400 newtons. Two: one fixed path and a rigid seat. Next: a floating seat, about plus or minus five degrees on springs with hard stops, so the cushion fits the hips, while total tilt stays under 16 degrees. Three: after standing, the fixed bar leaves no room for a walker. Next: a flip-up bar, latched and sensed before any lift, and a clip that holds the walker. Four: so far everything is 2D, quasi-static models. Next: a dynamic model and a prototype tested with healthy volunteers.
