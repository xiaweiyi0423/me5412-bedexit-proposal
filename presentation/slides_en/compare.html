We compared three products with ours on the same yardstick: can it be used at the bed edge, how far does the support surface rise, and does it move forward with the body. A bed rail works at the bed edge, but it does not move; it only gives something to hold. A lift cushion raises the seat, mainly on chairs or sofas. A power lift chair tilts the whole chair, but the help belongs to that one chair. Our seat works at the bed edge: on an inclined guide it moves forward and up together, about 185 millimetres forward and 274 millimetres up over the whole stroke. The lift chair's numbers with a star are estimated from its published tilt angle, not measured by the maker. Rated loads are defined differently by each maker; ours is a 100 kilogram design user, with the structure checked at one and a half times that. So the point here is where each one is used and how it moves.
