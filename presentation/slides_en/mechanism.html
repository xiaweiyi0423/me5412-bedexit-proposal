3 · MECHANISM DESIGN ③ INCLINED GUIDE + TILT LINK

One point on a line, one on an arc; twin guides take the side load

  • Idea: trace seat points along the path. Near-line point → carriage on guide (pivot 1); near-arc point → link to centre B (pivot 2)
  • Result: guide 34° from vertical, ball-screw stroke 331 mm, link 465 mm; seat centre within 8 mm, 0.8°; 18.5 cm forward, 27.4 cm up
  • 1-DOF: 3 revolute + 1 prismatic joint; motor → belt → ball screw → carriage

How a one-sided seat holds: load flows ①→②→③ to the guides; seat outer edge sags about 4 mm in total

Sized by differential evolution (cost = max deviation of seat centre and tilt from the design path). Cantilever loads are hand calculations (statics + beam formulas), not yet FEA; full check in the appendix "One-sided cantilever" slide

Step three is sizing. We move the seat along the design path and trace every point on it. A point that moves almost in a line rides a carriage on a guide. A point that moves almost on an arc is held by a link to centre B. So we search only 4 numbers. Result: guide 34 degrees from vertical, link 465 millimetres, error at most 8 millimetres. Three revolute joints and one prismatic joint: one degree of freedom. How does a seat held on one side carry the load? In the worst case, pivot 1 takes about 586 newton-metres. A pair of tapered roller bearings passes it to the two guides, with safety factors 3.2 and 2.5. The outer edge sags about 4 millimetres. These are hand calculations; next, we check them with FEA.
